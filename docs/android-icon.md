The APK uses a newly generated racing-track R monogram in ivory and teal, on a navy background. The source artwork and both prompts are in [assets/branding](../assets/branding/README.md). No original game artwork was used to create it.

I did not find a public licence allowing Sonic R's original sprites, textures, character artwork, or logos to be redistributed as an app icon. [Sega's streaming guidelines](https://support.sega.com/hc/en-us/articles/44194402112529-STREAMING-GUIDELINES) permit some use of gameplay imagery and official-site images for gameplay videos and their thumbnails, subject to conditions. That policy does not establish permission for an APK icon. This is a reading of its published scope, not a finding that every possible licence or legal exception has been ruled out.

The repository's [LICENSE](../LICENSE) permits downloading, building, and changing the code and prohibits selling it. It gives no express permission to reuse Sega's artwork. Owning the original game does not itself grant redistribution rights over its artwork. [UK copyright guidance](https://www.gov.uk/copyright) explains that copyright controls copying, adaptation, and distribution, including free copies.

The icon uses an independently generated shape, generic racing imagery, and a different colour scheme from the game's logo. It avoids character likenesses, copied type, and extracted images. This reduces reliance on Sega's artwork; it does not guarantee that the mark is free of all third-party rights. The app's existing name and the wider recompilation have separate rights questions.

Android 8 and later use the adaptive icon. Android 13 and later also have a monochrome layer for themed launchers. The foreground has proportional padding to keep the monogram inside the normal adaptive-icon safe area. The resources follow [Android's adaptive-icon format](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/system/icon_design_adaptive). They set the Android app icon.
